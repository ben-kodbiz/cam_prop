Methodology

This page explains how Open Evidence works. The project does not assume that any government, political movement, company, media organization or activist group is always truthful or always dishonest.

How claims are selected

Claims are discovered from public sources: official documents, court records, established news organizations, and social media. Social-media posts are used mainly to locate claims and original material, never automatically treated as proof.

How sources are ranked

  • Tier 1 — Primary: ICJ, ICC, UN bodies, official court records, government documents, parliamentary records, corporate filings, contracts, official company statements, verifiable datasets.
  • Tier 2 — Professional journalism: Reuters, AP, BBC, Financial Times, Guardian and other established outlets.
  • Tier 3 — Specialist investigations: academic research, investigative journalism, reputable NGOs, research institutes.
  • Tier 4 — Social media: claim discovery and locating original material only.

How evidence is evaluated

Evidence is scored on multiple dimensions — source quality, whether it is primary, independence, directness, corroboration, contradiction and recency. There is no simplistic true/false score. The score is evidence assistance, not truth itself; a human reviewer determines the final classification.

Possible outcomes

Supported, mostly supported, misleading, unsupported, contradicted, disputed, insufficient evidence, or unknown. "Unknown" is a legitimate outcome: if evidence is insufficient, that is what we publish.

Compound claims

Complicated statements are split into atomic factual assertions before evaluation. "X happened because Y and therefore Z is justified" becomes three separate claims requiring three separate bodies of evidence.

How AI is used

  • AI may summarize, classify, extract claims, compare evidence and draft explanations.
  • AI must not invent evidence, statistics, quotations or sources.
  • No source → no factual assertion.
  • High-impact topics (deaths, alleged war crimes, accusations against individuals or companies, legal conclusions) always require human approval before publication.

How humans review material

Every claim passes a review checklist before publication: is the claim correctly represented, is the speaker identified, is there a primary source, did we search for contradictory evidence, did we separate fact from interpretation, and is uncertainty clearly stated?

How conflicts are handled

When sources disagree, the claim is marked disputed and both supporting and contradicting evidence are shown side by side.

How corrections work

Every published record keeps a revision history. Corrections, clarifications, evidence updates, status changes and source removals are recorded in an append-only audit log. We never silently rewrite history.

Reproducibility

Every published assessment can be reproduced: claim ID, source IDs, URLs, retrieval dates, evidence excerpts, assessment, methodology version and review date are all available.

Open Evidence — evidence first, attribution always, uncertainty explicitly stated.
